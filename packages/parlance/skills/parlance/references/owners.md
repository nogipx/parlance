# owners: the code that owns a participant's words

## 19. Owners

A host's words have owners: one owner writes a state, serves a command, says a fact (§6). An owner is code a participant holds (§22.3): a class that extends the base its words' generator made (§23.4), and nothing else; what it does beyond its handlers it says through `OwnerTools` (`package:parlance/schema.dart`).

- **`IOwner`** (`schema.dart`, an interface with no members): what `main` gives a participant it makes (`host.participant(who, owners: [owner])`), once; it speaks as the owner named by its vocabulary (`home@house`), or, an owner of no words (a rule of the whole, `guardAll`), as the participant itself. It says nothing of who it is or what it may: `main` says that when it makes the participant. An owner extends a base the generator made (§23.4), which implements it and defines its words; that base says in its constructor `IOwner.made(this, define: (p) {...})`, the generator's contract, its words going with it. It has no member of the library's, so a name of its own is its own, in this version and those to come: what it does with the library it does by the extension **`OwnerTools`**, written as its own (`listen(...)`, `by`); where it has a member of that name, `OwnerTools(this).listen(...)`. It overrides nothing: what it follows, hears and lets go of it says in its constructor. A tool of more than one argument takes them named (`follow(pull: ..., on: ...)`), so a call says what each is; one of a single argument, what it runs, takes it as it is (`onDispose(f)`, `batch(writes)`, `apart(act)`); a guard's check gets one `GuardCall`, which may grow without breaking a guard written now.
- **`follow({pull, on})`**: for an owner whose states come from a model of its own (a device, a port): `pull` sets them from it, when it is attached, whenever `p.sync()` is called, and when `on` tells it the model moved (`Stream<void>`: a controller's listener, a device's callback), once for all told in one turn of the event loop and with the others of its participant told in that turn as one tick. Said once attached, it runs at once. What it sets equal to what is kept changes nothing.
- **`listen(on: ..., onData: ...)`**: listens once the owner is attached, for what its work reports on its own (a switch flipped by hand, a message come in) to write or say; the subscription goes with the owner. **`hear(fact: ..., onData: ...)`**: hears another's facts so (`IHouseClient.of(this).rang`, an `IFactOf`): what `onData` writes, says and calls follows from the fact heard, as a handler's follows from its call; no cause passed.
- **`choices(name: HouseChoices.room, list: () => [Choice(id, label: ...)])`** (a constant the generator makes of what the words name, §23): tells whoever asks the registry (an agent, an editor, a plugin) which rooms there are now, to choose from; a param or value of `@ChoiceOf('room')` is held to them (choices no one gives are refused, saying so). An app's own code needs none of it: it has its model. They are listed again on their own: when the owner is attached, after it followed its model, heard something or answered a call; what went goes of its keyed states keyed by them too, with no one removing it.
- **Another's words**: through their client made in the constructor, `IHouseClient.of(this)`, bound to the owner rather than to a participant: it finds the participant the owner is attached to when it is used, as that owner's part, here or on a guest. Its streams are made when they are listened to, so `hear` and `listen` take them at once; a read or a call before the owner is attached throws a `StateError`. Across a connection, another's state is kept only while it is watched: an owner that reads one `listen`s to its `changes`.
- **`whenAttached((p) {...})`** and **`participant`** (`OwnerParticipant`, in `tools.dart`, not `schema.dart`): what needs its participant itself, after its words are defined: a tool's, the raw participant (`p.raw`, words named at run time); at once when it is attached. **`participant`**: that part; before it is attached it throws.
- **`guard(id: ..., word: ..., check: (call) => why)` (a `GuardCall`: `call.params`, `call.by`, `call.command`)**: a guard (§10) of its own command, by the call's typed params and on whose behalf it comes; null lets it run. Told to the registry once attached, as `<part>#<id>`; who passes it is `check`'s to say (by its `by`), there is no override.
- **`guardAll(id: ..., check: (call) => why)`**: a guard of every command its participant serves, its own and those of the owners beside it, here and on a guest: a rule of the whole participant (who may call what beyond a word's callers), made by one owner; told as `<participant>#<id>`.
- **Availability, unsaid.** A command's `whyNot` that reads what the registry does not keep (a device, a port) is asked again after each `follow` pull and each event it `listen`s to or `hear`s, once in a turn; its status is told where it changed. No owner calls `availabilityChanged`.
- **`once(id: ..., say: () {...})`**: what it says there is said under `id`, so said again under the same id it is dropped, and `once` answers false (across a connection the host drops it): for what comes from outside more than once, a platform's event, a webhook retried. `newId()` gives a fresh id.
- **`now`**: the time by its registry's clock once it is attached (a test's, when it gives one), the world's before.
- **Words named at run time** (a device found, a plugin loaded) are a tool's, not an owner's way: `StateWordOf<T>(name, shape)` and `FactWordOf<T>(name, shape)` (`tools.dart`) are defined through the one door typed words have, `whenAttached((p) => GeneratedWords(p).state(StateWordOf(...)).define())`, in the participant's vocabulary.
- **What goes with it.** What an owner defined through its participant (its part) goes when it goes, by itself: nothing is kept by hand. **`onDispose(f)`** runs `f` then, for what it holds of its own (a timer cancelled, a device let go).
- **`by`**: who it answers where it runs: on whose behalf the call its handler serves came, or the fact it hears; null elsewhere. **`apart(act)`**: runs `act` as its own act, not an answer (a timer set in a handler to act later on its own). **`batch(writes)`**: writes as one tick outside a handler, as a handler's or a sync's are.
- **A participant holds its owners** (§22.3): `host.participant(who, owners: [Home(...), Garden(...)])` grants each owner's vocabulary and attaches it to the participant for it (`p.raw.ofOwner('house')`), so its acts are told as its own, in order, then writes what they have now as one tick; never two owners of one vocabulary, nor an owner attached already. No name is `main`'s to choose, so none drifts from the vocabulary a reader sees in the journal and in `Callers`, and no vocabulary is said twice. It is all or none: when one owner cannot define its words, the participant goes and the error goes on. A guest attaches its owners on its side (`part.attach([...])`), in the vocabularies the host granted it. `p.sync()` runs every owner's `follow` in one batch, so a change is one tick, seen whole, over a connection too; an owner whose pull throws writes nothing of it, the others write what they have, and the error goes on after. `p.dispose()` withdraws its owners, the last first, then all it did, for its owners too, and frees its vocabularies. There is no group apart from the participant: making one and forgetting the other cannot happen.
- **`Owner`**, an owner written by hand with `define(p)`, `sync()`, `changes` and `dispose()` to override, is the package's tests' own and not exported.
- **`failCall(why)`**: a handler whose device or port said no ends the call as `failed` with `why` (a `HandlerFailed`, told as it is, no exception type before it). A refusal with a code (`notPermitted`, `unavailable`, `denied`...) comes only from the registry's own stages (§9): to refuse before a call runs, give the command `available`.
- **`Handles`**: a set of handles to withdraw together, usable on its own (the package's test `Owner` keeps its handles in it, `keep`). `keep` after `dispose` withdraws the new handle at once and throws.

### 19.1 Words of others

An owner says nothing of the words of others it needs. It asks a word's status (§6.5) when it needs it, and reads, listens or calls as that status allows: an absent word fails nothing, so parts start in any order.

An owner whose model changes says where its changes come from (`on:`). A host whose model tells it changed some other way (a `Listenable`, a callback) calls `p.sync()` there: `controller.addListener(p.sync)`.

### 19.2 Scopes

A participant with its owners is a scope: `main` makes it when what it serves begins and disposes it when that ends, and what it held leaves the registry with it.

- **App**: for the life of the process (sign-in itself, sync, settings).
- **A user's session**: from sign-in to sign-out. Its participant is made on sign-in with the user's modules' vocabularies, and disposed on sign-out: its words, values, kinds and guards go, so nothing of one user reaches the next, whatever each module remembered to clear. Others see its words `missing` (§6.5) until the next session defines them.
- **A feature**: made when it opens (a route, a deferred import), disposed when it closes; outsiders see its words missing meanwhile.
- **Not in the registry**: a screen's own state, which no other part reads.

One word lives in one scope: a word two scopes need belongs to a participant above both, never to both (vocabularies are exclusive, §22).
