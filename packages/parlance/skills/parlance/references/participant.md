# parlance contract: participants (§22)

Part of the contract the code and its tests follow; sections keep their numbers across these files (`SPEC §n` in the code), and SKILL.md maps them. Where code and contract disagree, the contract is fixed first.

## 22. Participants

A **participant** is a part of the app as it speaks: it sees the registry only through an `IParticipant`, as one principal (`who`). `main` makes one per part and hands it over; the part never gets the `Registry`. So the callers and readers of words hold for it in this process as they do over remote: today any code with the registry can name any principal, a participant cannot.

The same interface serves a registry in this process (`LocalParticipant`, §22.1) and one across a connection (§22.4), so a part's code does not change with where it runs.

### 22.1 Making one

`host.participant(who, vocabularies: [...], rights: ..., access: ...)`: `Host` (`package:parlance/app.dart`, frozen) is what a host's `main` makes, a final class over the `Registry` of its process with `participant`, `serve` (a guest's session, `IGuestSession`: `who`, `ended`, `close()`), `alias`, `registerType` and `dispose`; its `registry` is there for the library's tools (a journal, MCP, an inspector) and promises nothing. A guest's `main` joins by `Guest.join(connection)` or `Guest.over(endpoint)`, an `IGuest`: the participant, its `errors` and `close()`. In tools and tests, `registry.participant(...)` (`package:parlance/participant.dart`) is the same participant.

**Frozen and raw.** What `schema.dart` shows of a participant, `IParticipant`, is what an owner, `main` and a client use: `who`, `child`, `attach`, `sync`, `settle`, `batch`, `dispose`, its reads and calls (`IRegistryView`), and its typed words (`p.state(word).define()`, `p.fact(word).define()`, `p.command(word).serve(handler)`, `p.query(word).serve(handler)`, `call`, `ask`, `read`, `watch`, `status`). Only the library makes one, so it may grow; nothing else implements it (a test fakes a client, `I<Class>Client`). Everything else below, by name or descriptor (`defineState`, `addGuard`, `registerKind`, `explain`, `clock`, a call with a cause, a typed `define` by its call object), is `IRawParticipant` (`package:parlance/participant.dart`, `p.raw`), for the library and its tools, and promises nothing.

- **Vocabularies** (§23): what a module's words are, each a `WordSet` (its name and its words, generated from `@Vocabulary`). Its name is the first words of its words' names: `orders` for `orders.cancel` and `orders.list.page`, and a word may be named as the vocabulary itself. Granting it writes the name once, where the words are; a word of it not named in it, or a name that is no name (§1), is refused (`ArgumentError`) when it is granted, every one at once, so a malformed word stops the app at start rather than when it would be defined. It is the only way a participant gets words to define. A vocabulary whose words come at run time (a plugin's, a guest's in another language) is granted as `WordSet.open(name)`: said so in `main`, never a vocabulary left out. One of hand-written enums is `WordSet('audio', [...SoundState.values, ...])`.

- **It defines only in its vocabularies**: a name in none of them is refused. Without vocabularies it defines nothing.
- **A name is one vocabulary's**: two vocabularies of one participant of the same name, or one inside the other (`orders` and `orders.archive`), are refused (`ArgumentError`), so the words named under a vocabulary are those of one owner and `orders.*` names exactly them.
- A vocabulary is **one live participant's**: one another participant has, or one inside or around it, is refused at once (`StateError`). It is free again when that participant goes.
- Words the host defines on the registry itself (sessions of §14) are not participants' and take no vocabulary.

### 22.2 What it may do

- **Read, watch, hear, call, ask a word's status** (§6.5) as `who`, through `IRegistryView` (§20) and `watch(state, key:)` (`watchAs(state, shape)` as a shape has it, §18): a state `who` may not read is null and watches as null; a fact it may not hear never comes. Its calls are refused as anyone's are (§9).
- **Define words in its vocabularies**: `defineState`, `defineCommand`, `defineFact` (each may take its values' shape, so the registry keeps them as their Dart values, §18; across a connection they go as JSON whatever the shape), or typed, `p.state(word).define()`, `p.command(word).define(handler, callers: ...)`, `p.fact(word).define()`; no `by`, it is `who`. A name in none of its vocabularies is refused (`StateError`). `rights.define(who, descriptor)` may refuse more (callers that name outsiders, a kind of word).
- **Write and say as itself.** A write, a call or a fact takes no cause, or one whose `by` is `who`: its own act, or what follows from a call it serves (`call.causeFor(who)`, and so by itself, with none given, in the handler of a call it serves). Another principal's cause is refused, but for a fact `rights.speakFor(who, by, fact)` allows: the fact's cause is then theirs, and so are the rights of what follows (a platform says a chat line for its viewer).
- **Say an open fact of another** (`producer(fact)`) only when `rights.produce(who, fact)` allows.
- **Guards on its own commands** (`addGuard`): a guard it adds judges only calls of commands it or one of its parts (§22.3) defined, never another's. The registry knows it as `<principal>#<id>` (`studio@platform#viewers.reporters`), so two participants' guards of one id are two guards, and a refusal and a call record say whose guard it was.
- **Entity kinds** (`registerKind`, §3) it keeps, and `entitiesChanged` of those only.
- **`availabilityChanged(command)`**: a check of its own command that reads what the registry does not keep answers otherwise now; watchers of its status are told (§6.5).
- **The core's interfaces** (§21.6): `state(word).reader()`, `command(word).caller` and `fact(word).listener` give the `IStateReader`, `ICommandCaller` and `IFactListener` of a word through it, so a part written against the core runs on a participant unchanged, here or across a connection (there another's state reads while it is watched).
- **`batch(writes)`**: its writes and facts in one tick (§7).
- **`settle()`**: completes when the registry has done all it asked so far, what it sees of the catalog (so `status` too) and of the states is as of then, and its listeners were told: what a test waits for after it acted, in place of a delay. In the host's process after a turn of the event loop; across a connection after the host answered, the catalog was asked anew and the copy reached the seq the host told then (its feeds are told the ticks they carried nothing of went by, as after a call).
- **`clock`**: where it reads the time; the registry's in this process.
- **`dispose()`** withdraws everything it defined, registered and added, and frees its vocabularies; its words are then missing to everyone (§6.5). What its code writes or says after it went (an `await` answered after) is not written nor said, and throws nothing: code still running when it went needs no check of its own. A handle disposed alone, while it lives, still refuses a write. `Registry.dispose()` disposes every participant left, a served one by its session.

**Access by default** (`WordAccess(read:, call:)`): who may read its states and facts and call its commands when a word does not say. A word's own `readers` or `callers` replace it whole, nothing merges. Without a default and a word's own, a state or a fact is everyone's to read, and a command is refused at once (`ArgumentError`): who may call it is never left to chance. It is applied as the participant defines, so it goes over a connection as part of the descriptor. A word kept from someone is `notPermitted` to them (§6.5), not missing, so a team sees whom to ask; there are no rights per field: what is private to fewer goes in a word of its own.

| Right | Null means |
|---|---|
| `define(who, d)` | every word in its vocabularies |
| `produce(who, fact)` | no open fact of another |
| `speakFor(who, by, fact)` | no one but itself |

### 22.3 Parts and owners

A participant has **parts**: `p.child(name)` speaks as `who.child(name)` (§4), in the same vocabularies with the same rights and access. What a part defines, writes, says and calls is told as the part's, so causes, call records and `explain` say which part of a module did it. A cause of its whole, passed to it (`call.causeFor(studio)` in a handler of `studio@sound`), is taken as its own; a sibling's is refused (`NotPermitted`). A part's guards judge only the commands that part defined, a whole's those of all its parts: the parts are it. One part per name, and a part has none (`child` of a part throws `StateError`, so owners attach to a whole); it goes with its whole, and going it withdraws what it defined. Across a connection a part's acts carry `as` (§14) and the host holds them to that part of the session.

A participant **holds its owners** (§19): an `Owner` is code that owns words; it defines them in `define(IParticipant p)` and says nothing of who it is or what it may. `main` makes the participant (its principal, vocabularies, rights, access) and attaches them: `p.attach({'sound': Mixer(...), ...})` attaches each to the part of its name, so every owner's acts are told as its own, and writes what they have now as one tick. The names are `main`'s, so an owner cannot pass for another. `p.sync()` syncs them as one tick, and `p.dispose()` takes them with it. The same owner attaches to a participant in the host's process or across a connection; `await p.attach(...)` completes when the registry has their words and what they set (across a connection once the host answered, what it refused told to `errors` by then).

### 22.4 Across a connection

`RemoteParticipant` (`package:parlance/remote.dart`) is the same `IParticipant` over a session (§14); its access by default is the host's: the participant the host serves is made with it, and `describe` tells it when it joins. It joins only over an `RpcClientConnection`: `RemoteParticipant.join(connection)` makes its peer endpoint on the connection's transport (`p.endpoint`, for other contracts on the same connection), starts the connection when it is idle, waits until it is online (a connection that gives up first fails the join), and joins again each time it is back. A connection that does not come back once lost (a wasm module's to its host, one in memory) is one whose factory gives its one transport and that does not reconnect (`shouldReconnect: (_) => false`); one that gives up is told to `errors` (`{op: join}`, `lost`). Over an endpoint made elsewhere that does not come back once lost (a guest's in a wasm or JavaScript runtime, handed to it by its runtime), `RemoteParticipant.over(endpoint)` is the same participant with no connection to join again over; the endpoint stays the caller's. `join` and `over` complete when its view is live: the feed's first snapshot has come. The host makes the participant as it makes a local one and serves it across the connection: `registry.participant(who, vocabularies:, rights:, access:).serve(endpoint)`, a `RemoteSession`. Only a new participant is served, and once: one served already, or one that defined, attached, guarded or registered anything in this process, is refused (`StateError`), since all it has goes with the session; the session is that participant in the host's process, so vocabularies are shared with the local ones and its rights are checked there. The participant learns its principal and vocabularies from `describe`.

- **What it asks goes in its order, and one turn of its event loop is one tick.** A define, a write, a fact, a kind, a guard, an availability: each returns at once (its handle, `true` for a fact) and is queued; the queue goes as one `apply` the host runs in one batch. A name in none of its vocabularies, or an act as another, is refused at once (`NotPermitted`); what the host refuses comes after, to `errors` (`ParticipantError`: the op, the session's status, why). `flush()` completes when the host has answered all it asked so far (and what an answer adds to it, the schemas of `built`), and what it refused of it has been told to `errors` by then; what is asked after the call is not waited for, so a flush ends however much is written meanwhile. When the session refuses a whole `apply` (one of its statuses, §14), each op is told to `errors` and the participant stays joined; any other failure is the way breaking: it is away, and joins again when the connection is back, or, while the connection says it is up, after a while, each time later (up to 30 s).
- **Its calls come after what it asked.** A call waits for the queue first, so it sees what the participant wrote; it answers once the mirror has reached what the call wrote (§14).
- **What it does in answer follows from the call.** A handler's writes, facts and calls (with no cause, or `call.causeFor(who)`) reach the host before the handler's answer, so their parent is the call being served.
- **It reads its own states as it wrote them, and another's as its mirror has them.** The mirror keeps what it watches (`watch`, `changes`), nothing else: a read of another's state it does not watch is null. A watch tells the first value once the mirror has it.
- **Its guards are asked in their turn.** A guard it adds is registered on the host as `<principal>#<id>` for its own commands, and the host asks it across the connection at the guards' stage (§9, §10); no answer within its time (2 s) denies.
- **Its commands' availability is data.** Its checks run in its process; when one answers otherwise (after a tick it hears, after its own writes, or on `availabilityChanged`), it tells the host, which answers `unavailable` with that reason and says so to watchers (§6.5).
- **A value is checked before it is sent.** A write or a fact its type does not take, or a key a keyed state lacks (or an unkeyed one is given), is refused at once (`TypeMismatch`, `ArgumentError`), as in the host's process; entities and the host's own types it cannot see, and the host checks them.
- **Its kinds are lists it sends.** `registerKind` sends the entities, `entitiesChanged` sends them again; the host keeps the last.
- **A lost connection is not its end, while the connection comes back.** While away it keeps what it defines and writes; only what it says waits to be sent. When the connection is back, the host's new session gets, in one tick: its kinds, its words, its own states' values as they stand, its guards, its commands' availability; then the facts it said while away (the last `keepAway`, §14.1), their ids letting the registry drop one it took before the drop. Its mirror resumes after the seq it has (§12). Calls made while away answer `lost` (§14). The host must have closed the old session first: its vocabularies are one live participant's (§22.1).
- **It goes with its session**, or by `close()`, which withdraws its owners (as a local participant's go with it), its words, guards and kinds and closes its endpoint; the connection stays the caller's to dispose.
- A fact's `true` does not say the host took it: a repeat of an id is dropped there.

### 22.5 A local registry

`LocalRegistry` (`package:parlance/participant.dart`) is the light way in: the words of an app in one process, keeping nothing past what is now. It makes participants as a `Registry` does, with the same `participant(who, vocabularies:, rights:, access:)`, `alias` and `registerType`, and they are the same `LocalParticipant`s held to the same rules: vocabularies, callers and readers, types (§2, §18: a typed word is kept as its Dart value here as there), availability, guards, causes and conversations, ticks, status, scopes. The owners, the words, the generated owner bases and clients and the parts that use them run on it unchanged.

- **It remembers nothing.** No ticks are retained, no call records, no recent facts: `recent` is empty, `explain` tells the value and no trail, `conversation` finds nothing. The journal (§9.3) is the registry's and not offered here.
- **Its commands are anyone's to call** unless a word or a participant's `access` says otherwise; a `Registry` refuses a command with no callers at all (§22.2).
- **Its participants are not served across a connection** (`serve` is refused, `StateError`): what goes across needs a feed to resume, and so a `Registry`.
- **Moving to a `Registry`** is `LocalRegistry()` made `Registry()`, and who may call the commands said where nothing says it; nothing else changes. `test/participant/levels_test.dart` holds both to the same promises.
